Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. Then you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or installing a new heater. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The trouble with water heater failures is that most of them start out of sight. Minerals settle into a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your unit has left.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Cary Homes

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our annual maintenance. On each scheduled visit, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, look over the gas valve and venting or the elements and wiring, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all matched to the heater in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Think of maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve ready to open, and that lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before recommending a new heater, Cary Water Heater looks at the details that determine your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a heater with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your busiest hour without paying to heat water you'll never use.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, and a well-sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive choice. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes in Cary run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Cary Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also verify the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
