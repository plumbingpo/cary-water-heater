Keeping Your Fuquay-Varina Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
