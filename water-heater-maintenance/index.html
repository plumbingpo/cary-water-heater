If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Cary, NC, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

The work is hands-on and specific. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and review stored error codes for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in Cary, call (984) 273-8748.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
