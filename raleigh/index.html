Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Raleigh

Many early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Raleigh

Around Raleigh, water heater trouble usually trace back to a handful of sources. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well may see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless unit on hard water should get annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor.
