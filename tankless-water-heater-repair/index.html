Tankless units are clever machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Cary, NC homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Cary home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Descaling is done with a pump and service valves, not by pouring vinegar into an inlet and hoping. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
